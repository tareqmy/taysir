# Taysir

Web app that teaches the Arabic of the Quran (vocabulary + grammar, from real verses). See `README.md` for commands and layout. This file records the decisions behind it and the rules to keep following.

## Decisions already made (do not re-ask)

- **Goal:** understand Quranic Arabic. Hybrid vocabulary + grammar, taught through real verses. A guided curriculum (not a free verse reader).
- **Learners:** a placement choice at the start: "new to the letters" or "can read the script". English only for now.
- **Stack:** SvelteKit 3 / Svelte 5 (runes) + TypeScript, client-only PWA (`ssr = false`). Progress is local-first in IndexedDB. No backend, no accounts yet.
- **Data:** Quranic Arabic Corpus (via the `mustafa0x/quran-morphology` fork), kept unchanged in `data/source/`. It is GPL, so **Taysir is GPL-3.0**.
- **Audio:** streamed from public CDNs (EveryAyah verses, Quran.com word audio). Never synthetic voice for Quran text.
- **v1 features:** spaced repetition (FSRS) and streaks with a daily goal. Not XP or badges.
- **More grammar:** a second grammar unit, `grammar-structure` (`src/lib/content/grammar-structure.ts`), sits right after `grammar-patterns` and before Juz Amma: small words before a noun (prepositions), sentences without verbs (with this/that, who and what), case endings and tanwīn, and “when”, “then” and “O”. Its examples come only from Al-Fatiha and surahs 105–114, which the learner has already studied, and `grammar-structure.spec.ts` checks that every Arabic word in it is a corpus word from those surahs. “Who/which” and “when” already had a short treatment in `grammar-patterns`, so these lessons build on it. Written after the Juz Amma units, where the examples are plentiful, as lessons of the `grammar-later` unit: runs of `idhā` (`grammar-idha`, from surahs 81 and 82; 84 is left out because its answer is less clear), conditions with in/law/man (`grammar-conditions`, from Juz Amma's short verses; `man` is a noun in the corpus, so the lesson calls them “condition words”, not small words), exceptions with illā (`grammar-exceptions`: the corpus tags illā after a group `EXP`, “except”, and after a negative `RES`, “only”, which the lesson follows), and oaths with wa- (`grammar-oaths`: the corpus tags the wa- that opens an oath `P`, a preposition meaning “by”, and the later ones `CONJ`, “and”, which the lesson follows). The six lessons are all written. The unit's prose checks: a spec that names two Arabic words side by side, or gives a word's meaning as `word (“meaning”)`, is checked against the verse data (`neighbours`, `glossClaims` in `testing/corpus.ts`), because a wrong example in an explanation passed every other check in an experiment. Comparing our grammar with an outside video workshop (Yusuf Mullan's “Read Arabic with Understanding in 21 Days”, March 2025; only about 5% of it was read, through its auto-captions, so treat a gap as a prompt to look, not as proof) found these gaps. It is a different method (framework first, then reading), so nothing is copied from it: write our own wording, with examples from the corpus, and send each lesson through the teacher review like the rest. (1) Written: `grammar-kinds` (`src/lib/content/grammar-kinds.ts`), the three kinds of word, noun (ism), verb (fiʿl) and small word (ḥarf, also called a particle, the Words page's name for it), is the first lesson of the `grammar` unit, so it comes before “the” and iḍāfa, which use “noun”. Its examples are only from Al-Fatiha, it says in one line that “you”, “who” and “this” are counted as nouns and never asks about them, and `grammar-kinds.spec.ts` checks every kind it names against the corpus's own tags. (2) Written: `grammar-past-persons`, she (-at), they (-ū) and the dual (-tā, from `dukkatā` in 69:14, the only dual past verb in the course's surahs), with one root, kāna, in three persons. (3) Written: `grammar-agreement`, a describing word matching its noun in ال, gender, number and case, taught as a rule (it had been only a note on two examples in `grammar-plurals`); it is the first lesson of the `grammar-later` unit below. Held back until the first review, because they are large: a catalogue of the other jobs a noun does (beyond the subject, object and after-a-preposition jobs in `grammar-cases`) and a system of verb and noun patterns (we teach four roots and the doer, done-to and “again and again” shapes, not the verb forms). Each is a product decision to put to the user as options first, and each joins a review part in `scripts/review-sheets.ts`.
- **Grammar from the later surahs:** a unit `grammar-later` (`src/lib/content/grammar-later/`, one file per lesson, `index.ts` builds the unit) that comes after all the surah units, so every example is a word already studied. It holds the lessons the notes above said were missing: describing words matching their noun, the other persons of the past tense, runs of idhā, conditions (in, law, man), exceptions with illā and oaths with wa-. It is review part 8, with `grammar-forms`. Its lessons are checked against the corpus's own tags by `grammar-later/*.spec.ts`, which use `src/lib/content/testing/corpus.ts` (a test helper that reads `data/source/quran-morphology.txt`: only specs import it, because it reads a file) and `grammar-blocks.ts` (the block builders the hand-written lessons share). A spec that names pairs of Arabic words in prose must also check that they stand side by side in a verse (`neighbours`, `sideBySide`): a wrong pairing in an explanation otherwise passes, as an experiment showed. `e2e/grammar-units.spec.ts` plays every lesson of the unit and of `grammar-forms` below.
- **Verb forms and noun patterns:** a unit `grammar-forms` (`src/lib/content/grammar-forms/`, after `grammar-later`, also in review part 8): Form II (a doubled middle letter) and Form IV (a- in the past) as “make someone do it”, Forms V and VI as “to oneself” and “to each other”, Forms VII, VIII and X (in-, a t after the first letter, ista-), and the noun patterns for a place (ma-), a lasting quality (a long ī before the last letter) and “the most” (a- in front). Every verb pair is a plain verb and a derived verb of the same root that the learner has met as vocabulary cards, and the specs check forms and roots against the corpus's `VF:` and `ROOT:` tags (`formOf`, `rootOf`, `lemmaOf`) and the noun shapes against the letters of the word. The corpus tags a few words against the usual reading (`tajallā` is tagged Form I, so the lessons use other examples), so check a candidate's tag before choosing it. `describeGrammarUnit` in `testing/unit-rules.ts` gives a hand-written grammar unit the shared checks: real Arabic only, neighbours in a verse, meanings that match the app's own glosses and roots that are roots of a taught word.
- **Arabic text size:** a Settings choice of four steps (Smaller 90%, Standard, Large 125%, Largest 150%), kept per device in `localStorage` (`taysir.arabicSize`), never in `Meta` or the backup, changed in Settings only, with a verse preview. One CSS variable, `--ar-scale`, multiplies every Arabic size.
- **Installing the app:** an “Install Taysir” section in Settings and, after a first finished lesson, a “Keep Taysir at hand” card on the home screen that “Not now” puts away for good (`localStorage` `taysir.installHint`, this device only). Chromium browsers get a button that brings up the browser's own prompt. A script in `src/app.html`'s head captures `beforeinstallprompt` (with `preventDefault()`) onto `window.__taysirInstallPrompt` from the first moment, because the app's own listener starts only after its chunks load; `AppInstall` (`src/lib/install.svelte.ts`) picks that up, or the live event, and keeps it until pressed. While the browser's prompt is open the state is `asking` (the button is disabled and nothing flips); a refusal sets `declined`; an iPhone or iPad gets three written steps, since Safari has no prompt. The iPhone copy says the home-screen app starts with empty saved data, apart from Safari's, and sends the learner to a backup. That claim is about how iOS keeps website data and has not been tried on a real iPhone: confirm it there. No offer shows when running as an installed app (`display-mode: standalone` or `navigator.standalone`); Settings then says so, and says “installed on this device” (not “using it as an installed app”) when the browser reports an install during a visit that is still a browser tab. When the card or the button goes, focus moves to `<main>` (or the Settings offer) and a live region says what happened. The manifest link has `crossorigin="use-credentials"` so it still loads behind Cloudflare Access. `e2e/install-hint.spec.ts` sends the page a fake `beforeinstallprompt`, since no test can wait for a real one.
- **A browser that will not save:** `AppState` falls back to an in-memory store, sets `storageProblem` and shows `StorageNotice` rather than hanging on “Loading…”; a refused save sets the same flag and never fails a learner's answer. Anything that saves must go through `persistMeta` or `save()` in `app.svelte.ts`, never `await this.store…` directly in an action. `restore` and `reset` (“Erase all progress”) are the exceptions: they throw, because each is an explicit action with its own error message, and carrying on after a failed erase would show an empty app that gets the old progress back at the next load. For the same reason the app never loads a page afresh itself (no `data-sveltekit-reload`, no `location.reload()` outside the update banner): in the in-memory fallback that loses the visit. A link to the page already open (“Keep going”, “Practise more”, the Review link at the top) keeps the page as it is in SvelteKit, so those pages start their next batch in `afterNavigate`; `e2e/blocked-storage.spec.ts` presses “Practise more” with storage refused.
- **Two tabs on one device:** each tab holds its own copy of the progress, so a save must never put back an out-of-date copy. `saveMeta` joins what it is given with what is stored (`mergeMeta` in `store.ts`, inside one IndexedDB transaction): finished lessons and days that met the goal are united, the count of exercises per day takes the larger, and the settings (starting point, daily goal, skipped lessons) stay as stored unless the save passes `{ settings: true }`, which only `setPlacement` and `setDailyGoal` do. `saveCards` never replaces a card with one that has been reviewed fewer times (`isBehind`). After a save `AppState.adopt` takes the stored result into the copy on screen, without taking anything away. What is not done: a tab does not hear of another tab's work until it next saves (it can still show an old streak or review list until then), the first answer from a tab that is behind adds to a count it has not seen (the larger count wins, so that one answer may not be counted), and an “Erase all progress” in one tab is undone if a tab that still holds the old progress saves afterwards. `e2e/two-tabs.spec.ts` plays a lesson in one tab while a second, older tab answers a question.
- **Hosting:** Cloudflare Pages, chosen but not set up (no account yet), with a trial copy behind Cloudflare Access and the public site left open (`hosting/README.md` is the checklist and the steps). `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` is run by hand only (`workflow_dispatch`), does nothing until the repository variable `DEPLOY_ENABLED` is `true`, and publishes to production only when "reviewed" is typed in and it is run from the default branch (its first step says why when it refuses); it is untested for want of an account. `hosting/_headers` sets caching on exact paths (never on `/*`, where a host joins values) and the safe headers. The rules a host must meet live in `scripts/hosting-check.ts` (a live site is checked with `make hosting-check URL=…`) and `src/lib/hosting.spec.ts` runs `hosting/_headers`, the trial-copy `robots.txt` and the workflow's guards through them. The app's routes must answer with `index.html`, so there must be no `404.html` in `build/`.
- **Trying it on a phone:** `make tunnel` serves the build and opens a temporary https tunnel (Cloudflare's quick tunnel by default, which needs `brew install cloudflared`; override with `TUNNEL=…`). A quick tunnel's address changes every run, so it cannot test an installed app or the update prompt, which need a fixed address; the README says so. `vite preview` answers 403 to any host name it does not know, so tunnel domains are listed in `preview.allowedHosts` in `vite.config.ts`, and `e2e/tunnel-host.spec.ts` guards that. Public hosting still waits for the teacher review; a tunnel is temporary and only as private as its address.
- **Browser tests:** Playwright, Chromium only, in `e2e/`, run against the production build and in their own CI job. They cover the learner journey, review and practice, restoring a backup, and an axe-core audit of every screen (phone and desktop, light and dark). `layout.spec.ts` checks every screen on a 375px phone and in a 1280px window at normal and 200% text with wide fonts pinned; the update banner (`update-banner.spec.ts`), the install offer (`install-hint.spec.ts`) and offline use (`offline.spec.ts`) have their own specs. `keyboard.spec.ts` presses Tab through every screen (`auditTabOrder` in `e2e/support/keyboard.ts`: every visible control reached, no positive `tabindex`, focus in view, outlined, uncovered, no trap) and does real journeys with Enter, Space and arrow keys.
- **Offline tests:** `offline.spec.ts` and `update-banner.spec.ts` get their server from `e2e/support/served.ts` (a copy of `build/` that `startSite()` can redeploy, stop with `down()` and restart at the same address with `up()`). Going offline means both `context.setOffline(true)` (so `navigator.onLine` is false, which the listening-question filter reads) and `site.down()` (so the service worker really cannot reach a server): either alone proves less. Playwright does not report the worker's own fetches, so a test that needs to know the app asked for a new version wraps `ServiceWorkerRegistration.prototype.update` in an init script. `seededLearner({ dueWords })` makes recent words due; `due` alone makes the first-learned cards due, which are letters and never get listening questions.
- **Progress page:** its own page at `/progress`, linked from the home streak card rather than the top bar (a fourth link wraps on narrow phones). Sections: words by how well they are known, Quran coverage, a 12-week practice calendar, and lessons per unit with streak records. Every number comes from `src/lib/progress/stats.ts`, which works from saved cards and `Meta`, so adding a statistic never needs a migration.
- **Your words:** `/words`, linked from the progress page (not the top bar). Learned vocabulary words only (a review card exists), never letters and never words from lessons not yet reached. Each row is a short line (Arabic, meaning, strength in words, speaker) that opens for the root, how often it appears and a verse; search by Arabic (vowel marks ignored, see `fold`), English or root, a strength filter, and a sort of newest (course order), weakest or most common, all in `src/lib/progress/words.ts`. The example verse comes from `wordExample`: the corpus sample is from surah 2 for a dozen words and the app has no verses or surah name for it, so those fall back to the first in-app verse holding the word, and four words (rahma, rahima, alim, ibada) have none and show only the reference. Never call `surahName` for a surah the app lacks: it throws, and it once crashed the page.
- **Extra practice and verses you know:** `/practice` drills the weakest words first and deliberately does not change the review schedule (it still counts toward the goal and streak), so answer it with `app.answer(undefined, …)`, never with a card id. `/verses` lists verses whose vocabulary words are all learned, from its own page linked from the progress page; a verse also needs at least half its words to be vocabulary words (`MIN_VOCABULARY_SHARE`), because 16 verses have no vocabulary words and 37 are mostly non-card words.
- **Look:** calm and respectful (warm neutrals, green and gold accents, Amiri Quran font, automatic dark mode).
- **Working style:** the user wants to pick from options for product decisions. Offer options rather than assuming.

## Rules

- **Never type Quranic Arabic by hand.** Take it from the corpus data. Lesson data points at corpus locations (`surah:ayah:word:segment`) in `data/lexicon-seeds.ts`, `data/fatiha-glosses.ts`, `data/juz-amma-glosses.ts`, `data/juz-amma/N.ts` and `data/juz-tabarak/N.ts`, then `npm run data:build` regenerates `src/lib/data/generated/`. Arabic inside lesson prose comes from `wordText(surah, ayah, n)`.
- English glosses and grammar explanations are **drafts needing review by a qualified teacher**. Keep explanations conservative; avoid claims you cannot stand behind (an earlier draft wrongly said a bare noun is always indefinite).
- Lesson content is validated by `src/lib/content/course.spec.ts`. Every new lesson or exercise must pass it (answer is among choices, no duplicate choices, ids exist). A vocabulary question never offers as a wrong answer a word whose meaning overlaps the right one's (“heart” and “heart, inner heart”), and a match never pairs two such words: `shareMeaning` in `exercises.ts` compares the comma-separated meanings of two glosses, and the question builders and the spec both use it. Two cards' meanings must differ, but they may overlap; when they do, they simply never meet in a question.
- Generated verse data ships in the app, so keep it small: words keep only text, gloss and `lexemeId`, plus `segments` (text and the tags `DET`, `PREF`, `SUFF`, `PRON`) only for words of several pieces. Read pieces through `segmentsOf(word)`. `course.spec.ts` caps its size; raise a field's cost only if the app reads it.
- Accessibility conventions are in the README (“Accessibility”). When checking layout in the browser, compare against a fixed width, not `innerWidth`: in phone emulation the viewport grows with overflowing content and hides the overflow.
- Show Arabic with `.ar-md`/`.ar-lg`/`.ar-xl` (or multiply a size by `var(--ar-scale, 1)`), never a fixed size, so the learner's Arabic size applies. The widest word in the course is about 3.5 times its font size wide and cannot wrap, so any grid or row holding Arabic tiles must derive its minimum column width from `--ar-scale` (see `ChooseView` and `MatchView`). `e2e/arabic-size.spec.ts` fails, naming the word, when one stops fitting at the Largest size on a 375px phone.
- "200% text" in a test means the browser's font size doubled, which `withBigText` in `e2e/support/layout.ts` does through Chromium's `Page.setFontSizes`. Setting `html { font-size: 32px }` from the page is not the same: a `rem` inside a media query (`@media (min-height: 36rem)` on the update banner, the `max-width: 26rem` and `30rem` breakpoints) means the browser's own setting, so the hack leaves those queries unchanged and tests a layout no learner sees.
- A bar fixed to the screen cannot be scrolled, so any of it that does not fit cannot be read. The answer feedback and the update banner are fixed (or sticky) only `@media (min-height: 36rem)`, which with 200% text means a screen about 1150px tall, and sit in the page below that; `layoutProblems` fails on a fixed element that does not fit on the screen, and `layout.spec.ts` shows the longest explanation in the course on a 375×667 phone.
- A grid or flex row of text must not let its column grow to its longest word: with big text that scrolls the whole page sideways (the About page and the welcome goal cards did, at 200% text). Give a text grid `grid-template-columns: minmax(0, 1fr)` and let its words break with `overflow-wrap: anywhere`; headings already break (`app.css`). `e2e/layout.spec.ts` fails, naming the screen, when one does.
- A control that goes away when it is used (a "Show all my words" button, a Continue button that is replaced) drops keyboard focus to the top of the page's Tab order, which can mean pressing Tab through hundreds of rows. Put focus somewhere sensible when you remove what has it (`clear()` in the words page focuses the search box; `focusOnMount` does this after an answer). A button or link must never have `tabindex="-1"`: `auditTabOrder` fails on it, and a heading that only a script focuses may.
- A lesson may only use examples from surahs earlier in the course order, because lessons unlock one after another. A new grammar unit should check that the way `grammar-structure.spec.ts` does, and corpus case labels (NOM, ACC, GEN, INDEF) can be read from `data/source/quran-morphology.txt` to keep an explanation honest.
- A question that has to be heard to be answered sets `listening: true` and an `audioUrl` (see `listenMeaning` and `verse-exercises.ts`). The runner drops these when offline and always offers a no-penalty skip, so never make hearing the only way to do something, and never time them for grading. The speaker button says “Hear the word” unless the exercise sets `audioLabel`; verse audio goes through `verseAudio` in `verse-exercises.ts`, which names it “Hear the verse”, and `course.spec.ts` checks the labels.
- Inside `src/lib`, use relative imports. Routes use the `#lib/*` alias (mapped in `package.json` `imports` and `tsconfig.json` `paths`).
- Rune-based state lives in `*.svelte.ts` files. Avoid TypeScript parameter properties there.

## SvelteKit 3 differences from what you may remember

- `$service-worker` is gone. Use `immutable` / `assets` from `$app/manifest` and `version` from `$app/env`. Manifest paths are relative to the worker, so resolve them against its URL.
- `goto(..., { replace: true })`, not `replaceState`.
- Svelte config lives in `vite.config.ts`. The static adapter uses `fallback: 'index.html'`.
- `tsconfig.json` must exclude `src/service-worker.ts`.

## Environment gotchas

- The shell here is zsh, which does not split an unquoted variable into words: `FILES="a b"; for f in $FILES` runs once on the single word `a b`. When breaking code on purpose to see a test fail, keep the originals with explicit commands (or a zsh array), check the backup exists before mutating, and `diff` against it after restoring. A mutation test once left eight breaks applied because the backup silently failed.
- In the Claude Code sandbox, npm cannot write `~/.npm`. Use `export npm_config_cache="$TMPDIR/npm-cache"` and allow `registry.npmjs.org`.
- `$state.snapshot` does nothing in the unit tests (Svelte compiles it for the server there), so a reactive proxy handed to IndexedDB or `structuredClone` only fails in a real browser (`DataCloneError`). Keep component data that is stored or cloned in `$state.raw`, and strip proxies in `AppState` before it reaches a store.
- A new service worker must not call `skipWaiting()` by itself. It waits for the page to ask (the `SKIP_WAITING` message), because activating deletes the old cache and would leave an open page running old code against missing files. The first install is the exception that needs no wait.
- `vite preview` indexes the files in `build/` when it starts. After `npm run build`, restart it (`make preview` does), or new hashed files 404 and the service worker's install fails without a clear error.
- The update prompt is covered by `e2e/update-banner.spec.ts`, which serves a copy of `build/` from `e2e/support/site.ts` and publishes a second version at the same address (a version differs by its id, which is in `index.html`, `service-worker.js` and `_app/version.json`). To try it by hand instead: `make build`, serve it, open the app in two tabs, rebuild (the version is a build timestamp) and restart the server, then click “Update now” in one tab and check that the other is not reloaded under you. Unregister the worker and delete the `taysir-*` caches afterwards. In the browser pane, `preview_start` reloads the tab it opens, so keep the tab you are watching separate from it.
- A locked lesson (one whose earlier lessons are not all done) shows “Not yet” at its URL, so a test or script that opens a lesson directly must first finish everything before it: `seededLearner({ lessonsDone })` in `e2e/support/seed.ts` does that.
- An axe check straight after switching colour scheme in a test can fail on the lesson buttons' colour fade (they transition their background), because axe measures contrast halfway through it. Wait for `document.getAnimations()` to finish first, as `useColorScheme` in `update-banner.spec.ts` does. The other audits set the scheme before the page loads, so they never see the fade.
- Browser tests (`make e2e`) start a server, and the Claude Code sandbox refuses local ports (`EPERM` on `listen`), so run them with the sandbox off or with `sandbox.network.allowLocalBinding: true`. `E2E_CHANNEL=chrome` uses the installed Chrome instead of a downloaded Chromium. When you add a screen, add it to `e2e/accessibility.spec.ts`, `e2e/layout.spec.ts` and the tour in `e2e/keyboard.spec.ts`; when you change a flow, run the journey spec, and break the app on purpose once to see that a new check can fail.
- The data modules import their JSON with `with { type: 'json' }`. Node insists on it, and the browser tests import `src/lib` straight into Node to know the right answers. Keep any module the tests import free of `$app` and Svelte-only imports.
- To audit a page with axe-core by hand, load it into the page from the browser pane: add a `<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/axe-core@4.10.2/axe.min.js">` from `javascript_tool`, then `await axe.run(document)`. It is not an npm dependency. The empty `color-contrast` “incomplete” entries for the lesson-list ✓ glyphs are expected: they are decoration with `aria-hidden`.
- Verify UI changes in a browser, not just tests. A useful trick in dev: from the page, `await import('/src/lib/content/course.ts')` gives the real exercise data, so a small script can answer every exercise correctly and confirm each lesson scores 100%. That caught a real bug (split words in the grammar view did not join).
- The in-app browser pane can report a 0x0 viewport (screenshots and `read_page` come back empty). Calling `resize_window` with a size gives it one. Progress lives in the IndexedDB database `taysir`: to try the review page, set `due` of the `cards` store to a past time and reload.
